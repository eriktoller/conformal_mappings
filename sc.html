Schwarz-Christoffel Mapping#

The Schwarz-Christoffel formula gives a conformal map from the upper half-plane to the interior of a polygon. It is especially useful because the polygon’s corners correspond to points on the real axis, called prevertices. Conformal maps preserve angles locally, and the formula encodes each polygon corner’s interior angle in the behavior of the map near its prevertex.

Let

\[\mathbb{H} = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : \operatorname{Im}(z) > 0\}\]

be the upper half-plane. For a polygon with vertices having interior angles \(\pi\alpha_1,\ldots,\pi\alpha_n\), choose real prevertices \(x_1 < \cdots < x_{n-1}\), with the remaining prevertex at infinity. The Schwarz-Christoffel map has the form

\[f(z) = A + C \int_{z_0}^{z} \prod_{j=1}^{n-1} (\zeta - x_j)^{\alpha_j - 1}\,d\zeta, \qquad z \in \mathbb{H},\]

where \(A\) and \(C\) are complex constants, and the powers are defined using branches analytic in \(\mathbb{H}\). Equivalently,

\[f'(z) = C\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(z-x_j)^{\alpha_j-1}.\]

Each exponent \(\alpha_j-1\) determines the angle at the corresponding polygon vertex. The angles satisfy \(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_j=n-2\), as required for a polygon, and the constant and prevertices are chosen to match its size, position, and side lengths.
