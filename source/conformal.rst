Conformal Mappings
===================

This is a collection of notes on conformal mappings: complex functions that
preserve angles locally. These maps provide a way to transform a geometric
problem in one domain into a simpler problem in another, while retaining the
local shape of curves and the angles at which they meet.

In complex analysis, a function is conformal at a point when it is
holomorphic there and its derivative is nonzero. Near such a point, the
function acts approximately like a rotation followed by a uniform scaling.
Thus, it preserves angles and orientation, but generally does not preserve
lengths or areas.

Conformal maps are useful for studying domains with awkward boundaries. For
example, a map can transform a region into the unit disk or the upper
half-plane, where questions about harmonic functions, potential flow, and
boundary values may be easier to solve. The following notes introduce several
examples and techniques, including mappings to wedges, quarter-planes, and
polygons.
